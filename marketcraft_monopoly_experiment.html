Economic Theory Spotlight

Price Taker vs. Price Maker

In Perfect Competition, individual sellers have zero market power. Because goods are identical and buyers have perfect information, pricing even a cent above market equilibrium drops your demand to zero. You must "take" the market price ($10-$12).

Pedagogical Manual For High School & Introductory College Economics

How to Run the 15-Minute "20-to-1" Experiment

Students rarely grasp that "monopolies restrict quantity to drive up prices" just by staring at textbook supply & demand graphs. This live activity creates genuine emotional realization: the anxiety of being undercut versus the sudden thrill of holding the sole monopoly card.

The Minute-by-Minute Classroom Blueprint

00:00 - 03:00

Card Dealing & Setup

Hand 20 students Seller Cards (Cost: $10). Hand the remaining 10-15 students Buyer Cards with random Willingness-To-Pay ($20 to $60).

Rules: Sellers want high, Buyers want low.
03:00 - 07:00

Round 1: 20 Sellers

Start 2:30 timer. Sellers scream deals to attract scarce buyers. Prices plummet to $10-$12 near marginal cost. Some sellers fail to sell.

Key: Intense competition erodes profit.
07:00 - 10:00

Round 2: 3 Sellers Left

Confiscate 17 seller cards. Announce: "A government regulation closed 17 firms. Only 3 licenses remain." Watch the 3 sellers quickly realize they can coordinate or charge $25-$35.

Key: Oligopoly & Collusion temptation.
10:00 - 13:00

Round 3: Pure Monopoly

Take away 2 cards. Exactly 1 student holds the market franchise. Line buyers in single file. The monopolist sets prices at $50-$65. Poor buyers are shut out completely!

Key: Emergence of Deadweight Loss.
13:00 - 15:00

Debrief & Projector Reveal

Turn on the projector graph. Compare the 3 rounds. Define Producer Surplus, Consumer Surplus, and explain why anti-trust laws exist.

Key: Economic intuition cemented.
📉

Price Taker vs. Price Maker

With 20 competitors selling identical goods, you have a horizontal individual demand curve. If you charge $10.01 while others charge $10.00, your sales drop to zero. In monopoly, you face the downward sloping market demand curve and choose your point along it.

🤝

Consumer vs. Producer Surplus

When 20 sellers compete, almost the entire economic surplus goes to buyers (they buy for $11 items they valued at $40). Under monopoly, the seller extracts this surplus, converting consumer surplus into monopoly profits.

🚫

The Evil of Deadweight Loss (DWL)

Why do economists dislike monopolies? Not just because high prices seem "unfair," but because mutually beneficial trades never occur. Buyers willing to pay $35 for an item costing $10 are turned away because the monopolist makes more profit selling fewer units at $60.
