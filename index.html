MARKET STRUCTURE GAME — MICROECONOMICS

Twenty sellers set a price. One seller sets it too.

Merge the market down from 20 competing sellers to a single monopolist, one round at a time, and watch price, output and welfare react — right up until the last seller standing changes the rules of the game.

RULES

How this works

  • The setup. One product, one group of buyers. Their total willingness to buy is fixed: P = 100 − Q — the higher the price, the less they buy.
  • The cost. Producing the product gets more expensive at the margin: the industry's marginal cost is MC = 10 + 0.5Q. This cost curve never changes — only who controls pricing changes.
  • Rule for 2+ sellers (perfect competition). No single seller can move the price alone — if they charge more, buyers just go to a rival. So every seller keeps producing until price equals marginal cost.
  • Rule for exactly 1 seller (monopoly). With no rival to lose customers to, the seller instead restricts output to the level where marginal revenue equals marginal cost — this lets them charge more by selling less.
  • Play the game. Start at 20 sellers. Click "Merge a seller" to consolidate the market by one seller per round. Watch the chart, the price, and the shaded areas as you approach the final round.
  • Read the chart. Grey line = demand. Amber line = marginal cost. Red dashed line = marginal revenue (appears only in monopoly). Teal shading = consumer surplus. Amber shading = producer surplus / profit. Red shading = deadweight loss — value that simply disappears.

THE MODEL

What's actually being computed

Demand (fixed)

P = 100 − Q

The same 100 buyers face the market regardless of how many sellers show up — only seller behaviour changes.

Industry marginal cost (fixed)

MC(Q) = 10 + 0.5Q

The true cost of producing one more unit, industry-wide. It doesn't change with the number of firms — what changes is who gets to set the price.

Perfect competition (n ≥ 2)

Set P = MC(Q)

Price-taking firms keep producing until price no longer covers the next unit's cost. Solving 100−Q = 10+0.5Q gives Q = 60, P = ₹40 — the same outcome whether there are 2 rivals or 20.

Monopoly (n = 1)

Set MR = MC, MR = 100 − 2Q

A single seller restricts output to raise price. Solving 100−2Q = 10+0.5Q gives Q = 36, P = ₹64 — less output, sold at a higher price.

Simplifying assumptions for the classroom: linear demand and cost, no fixed costs (so producer surplus = profit), and the same industry cost curve under both market structures — isolating the effect of market power rather than cost differences between the two worlds.
